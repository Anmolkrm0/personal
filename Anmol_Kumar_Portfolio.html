Turning scattered evidence into investigative leads

Officers had plenty of evidence. What they didn't have was a way to connect it. This is how we built one platform that does.

The problem

An STF officer working an organised-crime case reads FIRs and interrogation reports, scans thousands of rows of call and internet records, emails telecom companies and banks for more data, chases the replies, and then compares everything by hand.

It looked like a data problem. Sitting with officers showed it was really a connection problem: the information existed, but it was spread across documents, spreadsheets, inboxes and government systems.

What discovery changed

The brief was an AI tool for investigators. I mapped how a case actually moves through STF and interviewed every level of the hierarchy. Senior officers had a problem nobody had asked us to solve: no visibility into which cases were stuck or who was overloaded.

AdminSP and above. Needs visibility into every unit's caseload and progress.
Team leadCircle officers. Needs to allocate cases and balance workloads.
Investigation officerNeeds help analysing evidence and finding links fast.

So we built a role-based investigation platform with AI inside the workflow, not a standalone AI tool. That decision is a big reason all 9 units adopted it.

What we shipped

  • An agentic call-record analystI turned the questions officers ask most, such as "which number was contacted most often?", into tools for a LangGraph agent. Officers analyse huge telecom files in plain language instead of learning specialist software, with guardrails that reject out-of-scope questions.
  • Automated evidence requestsOfficers generate official notices from the panel. Replies from telecom providers, banks and ISPs are matched by reference ID and ingested into the right case automatically, removing days of follow-up.
  • CrimeGPTAn LLM-powered assistant that answers questions over a case's evidence in English, Hindi and Hinglish, grounded in the source documents. It cut data-discovery time by up to 90%.
  • Role-based dashboards and enrichmentCase allocation and progress tracking for leaders, plus automatic enrichment from vehicle, phone-number and facial and audio matching systems.

Decisions I'd defend

Ship documents before video.Video forensics was an exciting idea, but document processing and Q&A touched every case while video touched few and cost far more to build. We shipped documents first, and that is what drove adoption.
Keep every byte on STF's servers.For a security agency, data privacy wasn't negotiable. We chose open-weight models hosted on STF infrastructure and accepted more engineering effort in exchange for full control of the data.
Test in the language users actually type.Early accuracy was strong in English and weak in Hindi and Hinglish. I built a 600+ query multilingual benchmark and we added a translation layer, taking classification accuracy from 70–75% to 85–90%.

Saying no to a happy customer

A national café chain used Jarvis for footfall, demographics, occupancy and queue analytics. We built face-recognition attendance for their staff, and they loved it. Then they asked us to build a full employee-management system.

That would have pulled our team into HR software, a crowded market where we had no edge. Instead of a flat no, I asked what they really needed: attendance data flowing into HR. We exposed it through an API they could plug into their existing HRMS. They agreed, and we stayed focused.

A good no starts with the need behind the request.

How I think about product

Product and business aren't separate conversations. Each step only works if the one before it does.

  1. User problemStart with how the work actually gets done, step by step, before choosing any technology.
  2. Product valueRemove the slowest, most manual step. If AI is the right tool, make it reliable enough to trust.
  3. Customer valueLess manual effort becomes operational value the organisation can see and measure.
  4. Business valueWhen that value repeats across customers and use cases, it becomes a business.
